<Section icon={Wrench} title="Engine Parts: Disassembly and Replacement Workflow">

Engine work in Mechanic Legends is handled component by component. You select the engine part that needs service, remove it through the workshop interaction, and install the replacement, then continue checking the remaining components until the engine is restored or upgraded.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Bring the vehicle into the workshop">
    Enter the workshop area with the vehicle you want to improve and open the engine and vehicle component menus.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Inspect every engine component">
    Identify damaged, missing, or lower-performance parts before touching anything. This baseline makes later comparisons meaningful.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Remove the selected component">
    Choose the engine part requiring service and use the workshop interaction to remove it.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Install the replacement part">
    Fit the new component, then continue checking the remaining engine parts one by one.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Test and refine">
    Take the car onto the road and test launch response, acceleration, and top speed. Change one group of parts at a time so each adjustment is easy to understand.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning" title="Common Mistake">
Work through the vehicle systematically instead of switching between unrelated components. Jumping around the engine wastes time and makes it harder to tell which part fixed the problem.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Gauge} title="Racing Parts: Matching Upgrades to Race Type">

Racing-oriented car parts are most effective when selected as a complete setup rather than as isolated upgrades. A drag build, a drift build, and a multiplayer race build each ask different things from your components.

<CardGrid cols={3}>
  <Card title="Drag Racing Build">
    - **Prioritize engine performance and acceleration**
    - Stable vehicle that stays straight on launch
    - Few handling compromises on straight courses
  </Card>
  <Card title="Drift Build">
    - **Predictable handling over top speed**
    - Clear response to steering and throttle
    - Setup that recovers easily when the rear steps out
  </Card>
  <Card title="Balanced Build">
    - **Balanced acceleration and handling**
    - Reliable for exploration and mixed roads
    - Easier to control during jobs and travel
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

| Setup Focus | Key Parts | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Speed-focused | Engine upgrades, racing components | Long straights, drag racing | Weak in corners |
| Acceleration-focused | Launch-oriented parts | Quick launches, repeated starts | Lower top speed |
| Handling-focused | Suspension, tires, wheels | Drifting, corners, correction-heavy roads | Slower on straights |

<Callout type="success" title="Build Tip">
For multiplayer races, prepare both a speed-focused build and a balanced handling build. Routes mix straights and turns, and testing both setups before competing tells you which reacts better under pressure.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Target} title="Parts and Money: Upgrading on a Budget">

Parts cost Cash, and early spending mistakes slow your progression. Repair the vehicle to a usable condition before investing in performance parts, and put your first earnings into reliability and control rather than maximum speed.

| Stage | Part Spending Priority | Recommended Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early game | Condition, basic functionality | Repair work, standard jobs |
| Early-mid game | Handling, basic acceleration | Deliveries, taxi tasks |
| Mid game | Durability, travel performance | Reliable work vehicles |
| Mid-late game | Acceleration, top speed | Races and events |

<Callout type="tip" title="Reinvest Rule">
Use early rewards to improve reliability and control before chasing maximum speed. A dependable vehicle earns consistently; an underpowered race build loses more than it wins.
</Callout>
