Before buying any junkyard car, hold E to inspect it. The interaction panel shows damaged parts, overall condition, and the estimated repair cost. Compare that estimate against the car's market value, because a 20,000 car needing only 1,000 in repairs is a far better buy than a 22,000 car needing 3,300 in work.

| Inspection Factor | What to Look For | Green Flag | Red Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Estimated repairs | Total fix-up cost | Under **1,000** | Over **3,000** |
| Market value | Post-repair sale price | Well above cost + repairs | Thin or negative margin |
| Damage type | Which parts are broken | Cosmetic or single part | Engine or major component |
| Condition label | Overall status listed | Minor wear | "Absolutely ruined" states |

<Callout type="warning" title="Do the Math Before Buying">
Always subtract estimated repairs from market value before purchasing. Many junkyard cars look cheap but barely break even, or even lose money, after a full restoration.
</Callout>

<Section icon={Target} title="Step-by-Step: Repairing and Flipping a Car">

Once you own a damaged car, the restoration workflow follows a repeatable pattern. Drive it to your garage, lift it up, and work through every damaged component with your wrench in hand.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Diagnose at the junkyard">
    Hold E on a candidate car to view damage, condition, and estimated repair costs. Only buy cars with a healthy profit margin.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Lift and inspect in your garage">
    Drive the car into your garage, raise it on the lift, and open the trunk. Hover over each part with your wrench to see exactly what needs fixing, from batteries to intercooler damage.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Repair and polish every part">
    Charge or replace the battery, polish damaged parts like the exhaust manifold back to 100%, and super clean components when the option appears. Watch for the engine light, it should turn off when repairs are complete.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Repaint and detail">
    Nobody buys an ugly car. Strip the old paint at the paint station and apply a fresh color. This costs money up front, but it is an investment you recover at sale.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Sell for profit">
    Take the finished car to the sell point and cash out. Reinvest the profit into a better junkyard find and repeat the loop.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="success" title="Part Order Upgrades">
Before selling, check the part order menu for modifications and tuning options. Remaps and performance upgrades push cars past their stock limits and can raise resale appeal.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Sparkles} title="Tires, Rims, and Key Map Locations">

The tire machine is the one mechanic the tutorial skips entirely, yet it unlocks full wheel customization. Here is how it works: jack the car up, pull a tire off, place it on the splitter, and separate the tire from the rim. You can then buy a new rim, mount the tire onto it, and stick the assembled wheel straight back onto the car. Mixing and matching tires and rims is literally that simple once you know the machine.

Beyond the garage, several map locations keep your business running:

| Location | Purpose | Tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gas station | Refuel empty tanks | Free-ish upkeep, never skip it |
| Parts Direct | Buy engines and components | Use the teleporter to travel faster |
| Dealer tire route | Change tires and rims | Requires the splitter machine |
| Paint shop | Strip and repaint cars | Costs money, recovered at sale |
| Sell point | Offload finished cars | Compare price vs. your total spend |

<Callout type="info" title="Powerful Engines Are an Upgrade Path">
Parts Direct stocks a wide range of engines. Swapping in a more powerful engine transforms a mediocre flip car into a genuinely fast machine, and sometimes into a keeper worth holding.
</Callout>

</Section>
