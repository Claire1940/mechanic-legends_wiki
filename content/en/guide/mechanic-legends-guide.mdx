The junkyard is where real money is made. When you interact with a junkyard car (hold E), you can see its damaged parts, overall condition, repair estimate, and market value. The math is simple:

| Scenario | Purchase Price | Est. Repairs | Potential Profit | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overpriced wreck | 22,000 | 3,300 | Very thin | Skip |
| Budget find | 6,000 | 1,000 | Healthy margin | **Buy** |
| Starter car sale | Free | N/A | +25,000 | **Sell immediately** |

<Callout type="warning" title="Check the Repair Estimate First">
Never buy a junkyard car without checking the estimated repair cost. A cheap purchase price means nothing if repairs eat the entire margin. A 1,000-coin repair car often beats a flashier 20,000-coin wreck.
</Callout>

<Section icon={Target} title="Step-by-Step: Repair and Flip a Car">

The full refurbishment workflow takes a car from wreck to showroom condition. Follow these steps in order for the fastest turnaround:

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Buy From the Junkyard">
    Interact with a car (hold E), check damaged parts, condition, and repair estimate. Pick cars with low repair costs relative to market value, then drive it back to your garage.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Lift and Inspect">
    Put the car on the lift and open the trunk. Hold out your wrench to highlight every damaged component — battery, radiator, intercooler, exhaust manifold, and more.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Repair and Polish Parts">
    Charge or swap the battery, polish damaged parts back to 100%, and reinstall everything. Hover over each part to confirm whether it needs replacement or just cleaning.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Handle Tires and Rims">
    Use the splitter machine: remove a tire, split it, swap the rim if desired, then remount and reinstall. This process is not covered in the tutorial, so practice it once on a cheap car.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Paint and Sell">
    Strip and repaint the car — nobody buys an ugly car. It costs money upfront, but the resale value recovery makes it an investment, not an expense. Then sell for profit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="success" title="Engine Light Off Means Ready">
Once repairs are complete, the engine light turns off — that is your signal the car is mechanically sound and ready for paint and resale.
</Callout>

</Section>
