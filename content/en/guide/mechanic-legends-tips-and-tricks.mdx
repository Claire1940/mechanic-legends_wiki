<Section icon={DollarSign} title="How to Pick Profitable Junkyard Cars">

The junkyard is the heart of making money in Mechanic Legends. Its inventory refreshes roughly every 12 minutes, so each rotation is a new opportunity. The single most important habit is checking two numbers on every car before you buy: the **purchase price** and the **estimated repair cost**.

Follow this evaluation framework:

| Junkyard Car Factor | What to Look For | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase price | Lower than your budget with room to spare | Leaves cash for repairs and paint |
| Estimated repairs | Ideally under **1,000-3,000** | High repair bills destroy profit margins |
| Market value | Well above price + repairs | The gap is your profit |
| Damage type | Cosmetic over mechanical | Polishing and paint are cheaper than engine swaps |

A car priced at 22,000 with 3,300 in estimated repairs often yields barely any profit. A cheaper car needing only about 1,000 in repairs is usually the far better deal, even if it looks rougher on the lot.

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid the Profit Trap">
Do not fall in love with expensive junkyard cars just because they look fast. If purchase price plus estimated repairs comes close to market value, you will spend ten minutes of work for almost nothing. Always do the math before buying.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Auction Warning">
The auction garage is effectively gambling — you pay a fixed price for a random car that could be terrible or extremely rare. It can pay off big, but for steady income, stick to junkyard flipping until you have a large cash cushion.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Map} title="Step-by-Step Car Flipping Workflow">

Once you have bought a junkyard car, follow this repeatable workflow to turn it into profit. The full repair-to-sale process takes place across your garage, the paint shop, and the tire station.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Tow It to Your Garage">
    Drive or deliver the damaged car to your garage and lift it up. Open the trunk and hood so every component is accessible.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Diagnose With Your Wrench">
    Hold out your wrench and hover over each part. The overlay shows the damage level and condition of every component — battery, radiator, engine, intercooler, exhaust manifold, and more.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Repair and Polish Parts">
    Charge or replace the battery, fix broken parts, and use the super-clean and polish options to push components back to **100%**. Many parts only need polishing, which is far cheaper than replacement.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Repaint the Body">
    Take the car to the paint shop, strip the old paint, and apply a fresh color. Stripping paint costs a lot up front, but it is an investment you recover at sale — nobody buys a car that looks wrecked.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Sell and Reinvest">
    Sell the finished car, count your profit, and immediately reinvest in the next junkyard refresh. Repeating this loop compounds your cash faster than any single flip.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="success" title="Efficiency Tip">
You can mix and match tires and rims at the tire station using the splitter and mounting machine — a process the tutorial never explains. Jack up the car, pull the tire, split it, swap the rim, mount, and refit. Fully refreshed wheels add real value to every sale.
</Callout>

</Section>
