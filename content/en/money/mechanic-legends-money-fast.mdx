<Section icon={Map} title="Junkyard Flipping: Choosing the Right Car">

The junkyard is the heart of making money fast in Mechanic Legends. Not every car is worth buying — the goal is finding the biggest gap between purchase price and market value.

| What to Check | What to Look For | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Damage parts count | Cars needing only 2-3 parts | Fewer parts = lower repair cost |
| Market value | High relative to asking price | Bigger profit margin |
| Year of the car | Newer models preferred | Newer cars tend to sell for more |
| Part condition | Scrapped vs. repairable | Scrapped parts must be replaced, not polished |

A 9,000-cash junkyard car worth 9.2k at market value but needing only two cheap parts can be an excellent bargain — the profit comes from the spread, not the sticker price.

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid This Mistake">
Do not assume the most expensive junkyard car is the best deal. Always compare the **market value** against the price and count the damaged parts before buying.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Target} title="Step-by-Step Flip Routine">

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Buy a bargain at the junkyard">
    Check the damage parts count and market value. Prioritize cars needing only 2-3 replacements with a strong value gap.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Diagnose and remove damaged parts">
    Raise the car, open the hood, and pull the battery, radiator, and engine components. Charge the battery and clean or polish anything repairable.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Order replacement parts">
    Visit the parts store and order what's scrapped — engine blocks, exhaust manifolds, wiring harnesses. While waiting, polish remaining parts and accept customer jobs.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Reassemble and repaint">
    Install the new parts, then follow the GPS to the paint shop. Strip old rusted paint and apply a fresh coat in the paint booth.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Sell at the dealership">
    Drive the finished car to the dealership and sell at full market value — or keep it as your personal upgrade.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="success" title="Keep or Sell?">
You don't have to sell every car you repair. If a junkyard find has better specs than your current ride, keeping it can be cheaper than buying from the dealership outright.
</Callout>
