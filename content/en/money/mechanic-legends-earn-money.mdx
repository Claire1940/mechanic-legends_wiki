<Section icon={Map} title="Junkyard Bargain Hunting">

The junkyard is where real profits begin. Each wreck shows its price, market value, model year, and the number of damaged parts. Your job is to find the widest gap between price and market value with the fewest parts needed.

<CardGrid cols={3}>
  <Card title="Best Value Picks">
    - **Few damaged parts** (2-3 max)
    - High market value vs price
    - Cheap parts like wiring or manifolds
  </Card>
  <Card title="Risky Picks">
    - Many damaged parts (6-7)
    - Engine block and transmission both shot
    - Older model years
  </Card>
  <Card title="Keeper Picks">
    - Good top speed stats
    - Solid body condition
    - Worth upgrading instead of selling
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<Callout type="warning" title="Read Before You Buy">
Always compare the **market value** against the purchase price before committing. A cheap wreck needing an engine block and exhaust manifold can still be profitable, but only if the market value covers both parts and labor.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Wrench} title="Repair Workflow Step-by-Step">

Every flip follows the same repair sequence in your garage. Master it and you can turn a wreck around in minutes.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Lift and Diagnose">
    Raise the car, open the hood, and inspect each component. Damaged parts are highlighted so you can build a shopping list before spending anything.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Remove and Service Parts">
    Pull the battery and put it on the charger. Clean dirty parts and polish corroded ones. Scrapped parts cannot be saved — order replacements.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Order Replacement Parts">
    Drive to the parts store and buy what you need, such as engine blocks, exhaust manifolds, or a wiring harness. While waiting, keep polishing salvageable parts.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Reassemble and Repaint">
    Install all parts, close the hood, and lower the car. Strip old paint at the paint shop and apply a fresh color — a clean paint job raises the sale price.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Sell at the Dealership">
    Drive the finished car to the dealership and sell it. Reinvest your profit into the next, better junkyard bargain.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="success" title="Efficiency Boost">
Batch your repairs: while one part charges or polish orders ship, service other components at the same time instead of standing idle.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Target} title="Upgrades: Invest or Flip?">

Not every car should be sold. Upgrading a car you keep makes it faster, better handling, and more enjoyable to drive — but upgrades cost cash that could fund more flips.

| Upgrade Type | Effect | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New engine | **Higher top speed** | Keepers and racers |
| Modifications | Performance tuning | Cars you plan to keep |
| Wiring harness | Restores power | Any non-starting flip |
| Paint job | Higher resale value | Every car you sell |
| Battery charge | Required to start | Every repair |

<Callout type="info" title="Rule of Thumb">
Flip first, upgrade later. Early on, your cash grows fastest by cycling junkyard cars. Once you have a comfortable buffer, buy one solid car and invest in engines and tuning.
</Callout>
