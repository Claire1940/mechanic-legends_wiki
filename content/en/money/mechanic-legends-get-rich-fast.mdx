<Section icon={Map} title="Where to Get Cars: Sources Compared">

Every car you flip comes from one of four sources. Each carries a different risk and reward profile, so match your choice to your bank balance.

| Source | Cost | Risk | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Junkyard** | Low purchase + repairs | Low | Reliable flipping, steady profit |
| **Auction** | Garage bid price | High | Gambling for rare cars |
| **Dealership** | Full retail price | None | Personal cars you keep |
| **Customer Jobs** | None | None | Steady income, no investment |

**Key notes on each source:**

- **Junkyard**: Cars reset roughly every 12 minutes. Inspect condition and damage parts by holding E on a vehicle before buying. This is your bread-and-butter flipping source.
- **Auction**: Buying a garage gives you a random car — you might get something terrible or something extremely rare. Treat it as entertainment, not a business plan.
- **Dealership**: Brand-new cars with no hidden damage. Use this once you have money and want a keeper.
- **Customer jobs**: Repair and modify client cars and push them to the limit — steady income with zero upfront investment.

<Section icon={Wrench} title="Step-by-Step: Flipping a Junkyard Car">

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Pick a Low-Repair Car">
    Go to the junkyard, hold E on candidate cars, and compare condition, damage parts, and repair estimates. Choose the car where repairs are minimal relative to market value.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Diagnose in Your Garage">
    Drive the car to your garage, lift it up, and open the hood and trunk. Hold out your wrench and hover over each part — the tooltip shows whether a part needs changing, charging, or just polishing.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Repair and Polish">
    Fix damaged components like batteries, intercoolers, and exhaust manifolds. Use super clean and polish options to push part condition toward 100% — condition directly affects sale value.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Strip and Repaint">
    Take the car to the paint station and strip the old paint. Repainting costs money, but it is an investment you recover in the sale price — nobody buys an ugly car.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Sell and Reinvest">
    Sell the finished car, then immediately reinvest the proceeds into the next low-repair junkyard find. Consistent cycles compound your cash fast.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="success" title="Efficiency Tip">
Repair costs are front-loaded at purchase. Doing all the wrench work yourself instead of skipping steps keeps more of the sale price in your pocket.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Car} title="Must-Know Mechanics the Tutorial Skips">

Several systems in Mechanic Legends are either skipped or barely covered by the tutorial. Mastering them saves you time and money.

| Mechanic | Location | What It Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tire Splitter** | Dealer route area | Removes tires, splits them from rims |
| **Tire Mounter** | Same station | Mounts new rims and refits tires |
| **Parts Direct** | Teleport-accessible shop | Buy engines and performance parts |
| **Gas Station** | Map marker | Refuel cars that run dry |
| **Part Order** | Garage menu | Order modifications and tuning parts |

**Tire work explained**: Jack the car up, pull the tire off, place it on the splitter to separate tire from rim. Buy the rim you want, mount the tire back onto it, and stick the assembly back on the car. It is simple once you have done it once — the game never walks you through it.

**Upgrades worth ordering**: Remaps and other tuning parts from the part order menu make your car sound better and go faster. These also raise the value of cars you plan to sell.

<Callout type="info" title="Teleporters Save Time">
Use the teleporter to jump straight to Parts Direct instead of driving across the map. Time saved is money earned when you are running multiple flips per session.
</Callout>
