The Mechanic Legends car dealership system turns you into a one-person used-car business. You start at the junkyard, buy abandoned cars for around 5,000 bucks, tow them to your garage, repair every mechanical component, and then drive the finished vehicle to the port where Roberto buys it. Your profit margin depends entirely on how cheaply you source the car and how few replacement parts it needs.

| Step | Location | Action |

<Section icon={Wrench} title="Repairing Cars: Parts, Machine, and Temperature">

Every mechanical component in Mechanic Legends has a condition percentage. Half-life parts perform poorly — a car with a 50% engine can barely reach 70 km/h. Use the repair machine in your garage to restore parts to 100%, then reinstall them one by one.

| Component | Base Condition (Typical) | Repairable? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Engine block | 50% | Yes (or buy new, 9,000) | Can catch fire and be destroyed |
| Battery | 50% | Yes, via charging machine | Cheap to restore |
| Radiator | Missing | No, must buy (20,000) | Keeps temp locked near 90 degrees |
| Injectors/nozzles | Varies | Some yes | Test before removing |
| Oil | Low | Buy refill (expensive) | Affects engine health |

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Open the Hood and Strip Parts">
    Use your key to remove the engine, battery, and injectors. Lay each part out so nothing gets lost under other components.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Repair Each Part">
    Move every part to the repair machine. Each cycle restores a component to 100%. Charge the battery on its dedicated charger.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Reassemble Carefully">
    Reinstall everything and double-check the floor for leftover parts. A missing engine block means the car will not start at all.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Monitor Temperature">
    Watch the gauge. Without a radiator, the car climbs past 130 degrees quickly and explodes at 160.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning" title="Engine Fires Are Real">
If the temperature hits 160 degrees, the engine catches fire. Act fast: get to your garage, open the hood, and strip the burning parts. If the engine block burns up, it disappears and you must buy a brand-new one for 9,000 bucks.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={DollarSign} title="Dealership Economics: When to Sell and When to Keep">

The biggest mistake new mechanics make is ignoring total costs. A 5,000 junkyard car plus 9,000 paint and a 9,000 engine totals 23,000 — if Roberto only offers 14,000, selling means a loss.

| Cost Item | Price (Bucks) | Required? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Junkyard car | ~5,000 | Yes |
| Full paint job | ~9,000 | Optional, boosts value |
| New 4-cylinder engine | 9,000 | Only if block destroyed |
| Radiator | 20,000 | Strongly recommended |
| Engine oil refill | ~10,000 | Optional |
| V8 engine block | 35,000 | Luxury upgrade |
| Turbo/intake | ~100,000 | Endgame tuning |

<Callout type="info" title="The Keep-or-Sell Rule">
Before driving to the port, add up your purchase plus all parts and paint. If Roberto's likely offer is below that total, keep the car in your garage collection and only sell clean, low-cost flips.
</Callout>

In practice, a simple mechanical restoration with no radiator and no paint is the most profitable flip: buy at 5,000, repair everything in-house, and sell near 9,600. Heavily customized cars like a painted Peugeot 206 often cost more to build than they fetch.

</Section>
