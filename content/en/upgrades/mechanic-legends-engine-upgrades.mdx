<Section icon={Wrench} title="How Engine Upgrades Work in Mechanic Legends">

Mechanic Legends treats engine work as a hands-on workshop process. You inspect the vehicle, remove the existing component, install a replacement, and repeat until the engine is either restored or upgraded. Every performance characteristic of your car — acceleration, top speed, and how it behaves in races — is shaped by the parts you choose and how they work together.

The workshop system separates two different tasks that many new players confuse:

| Task | Goal | When to Do It |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engine repair | Restore damaged or missing components | Before any performance upgrades |
| Engine upgrade | Replace stock parts with higher-performance components | After the vehicle is fully functional |
| Racing parts | Specialize the car toward drag, drift, or competition | Once income is stable |

<Callout type="warning" title="Repair Before You Upgrade">
Installing racing components on a damaged engine wastes money. A vehicle in poor condition reduces efficiency and makes early activities harder to complete. Restore full functionality first, then invest in performance.
</Callout>

<Section icon={Gauge} title="Engine Upgrade Order: Step-by-Step Workshop Flow">

Follow this workshop sequence for any vehicle you plan to upgrade. The order matters — skipping inspection leads to unnecessary part changes, and skipping testing leaves you guessing which upgrade actually helped.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Inspect Before Changing Parts">
    Bring the vehicle into the workshop and review the available engine and vehicle components. Identify damaged, missing, or lower-performance parts before touching anything. This baseline makes every later comparison meaningful.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Remove and Replace Engine Components">
    Select the component that needs service, remove the existing part through the workshop interaction, and install the replacement. Work component by component through the engine until it is fully restored.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Install Racing-Oriented Parts">
    With the engine healthy, install performance parts that match your intended activity. Prioritize engine upgrades for straight-line speed and acceleration, and avoid sacrificing too much control on routes with turns.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Balance Speed, Acceleration, and Handling">
    Tune the setup for the environment: speed-focused for long straights, acceleration-focused for launches and repeated starts, handling-focused for drifting and corners.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Test and Refine">
    Take the car onto the road after each major change. Test launch response, acceleration, and steering stability. Change one group of parts at a time so the effect of each adjustment stays readable.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="tip" title="Pro Tip: One Change at a Time">
If you swap the intake, exhaust, and tires simultaneously and the car feels worse, you cannot tell which part caused it. Group related changes, test, then adjust — this habit turns trial and error into a reliable tuning method.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Map} title="Tuning Setups by Activity Type">

No single upgrade path wins everywhere. The right engine and tuning configuration depends on what you actually drive.

| Setup | Prioritize | Best For | Trade-Off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Speed-focused | Top speed, sustained power | Long straights, drag racing | Weaker launches and cornering |
| Acceleration-focused | Launch response, quick starts | Drag launches, stop-and-go routes | Lower top-end speed |
| Handling-focused | Predictable steering, control | Drifting, technical corners | Reduced peak speed |
| Balanced | Even spread of all attributes | Exploration, mixed roads, taxi work | No single standout strength |
