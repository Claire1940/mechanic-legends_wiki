<Section icon={Map} title="Where the ECU Fits in Mechanic Legends">

In Mechanic Legends, your core gameplay loop is buying junk cars from the junkyard, repairing them, and flipping them for profit. The ECU (Engine Control Unit) is one of the electronic components that can fail on a junk car, preventing the engine from running properly. Understanding how to buy an ECU is essential because a car with a dead ECU will not start, and Alfredo, the buyer, pays far less for non-running vehicles.

The parts supply chain in the game runs through the auto parts store (the large shop stocked with fluids, engine parts, and consumables). Everything you need for a repair — from coolant and engine oil to spark plug wires and electronics — is purchased there with in-game cash.

<Callout type="warning" title="Read Before You Buy">

<Section icon={Wrench} title="Installation Tips and Common Mistakes">

Installing the ECU is straightforward, but the surrounding repair workflow is where players lose money. These practices keep your repair smooth:

- **Clean parts first**: Blocks, heads, and exhaust manifolds go in the machine grinder; most other components go in the sink. Cleaned parts return to 0% wear.
- **Replace wear items**: New spark plug wires and related electrical components help push the engine toward a "perfect" running condition.
- **Watch the temperature gauge**: Above roughly 120°C, shut the car off immediately. Overheating can destroy the engine and erase your investment.
- **Skip the gas for flips**: If a car is only being repaired and sold, do not fill the tank. Fuel is expensive and adds nothing to the resale price.
- **Do not lose parts**: Carrying loose components between locations risks dropping them. Install parts promptly rather than stacking them on roofs or workbenches.

<Callout type="warning" title="Blown Engine Math">
If a project engine explodes, weigh the replacement cost against simply selling the car as-is. Alfredo accepts incomplete cars, and a new engine block often costs more than the car's remaining value.
</Callout>

</Section>
